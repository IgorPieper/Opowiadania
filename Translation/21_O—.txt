O—

An old oak stands in the park, a silent witness from dawn till dark. No one asks where it came from, no one wants to know. Time goes by, it outlives us all, that's just the way things go. Who planted it? No one can say. That memory faded long away.

Day after day, no one gives it a glance. Children run laughing, while grown-ups bite their lips. Only one old lady sits nearby, spending whole afternoons letting old sorrows fly. She feeds as many pigeons as days in grief she has left in life. And yet, not long ago, she happily drove herself into the mistakes of the days to come.

Here, where you run, shamelessly piss, loudly disturb my sleep, a place of mourning once drew people from far and wide. Here I saw seas of blood, monuments shining bright, polished by a dozen tears. And they, once young, fell into ruin until the world forgot them and turned away.

I've touched so many of you beneath, as my roots grew strong through your forefathers' teeth. Your grandfathers, their grandfathers, all buried below, and I used to wonder who'd be the next to go. Now I understand, like mushrooms on the forest floor, your faces will blend into muddy mush, and be no more.

And I too shall fade, full of regret at the end of my days, recalling my youth through memory's haze. My frail, supple stem once struggled to rise, racing for the endless light from the sky. And what was it all for, that hunger to grow, if another being will break me down below?

At least I'll leave with a grin, as death closes in, seeing that hideous face spray-painted on Bogdan's bark. My neighbor will surely outlive those who left that mark, growing beyond their time, still standing in the park.
