This used to be a field.
The meadow stretched from east to west, reaching all the way to the horizon.
Animals used to run here, a little river used to flow.
A gentle breeze was a delight to everyone.
The pastures were established, someone hauled away the hay, a barn was built, and the owners went broke.
A visionary bought the land, divided every plot, and marked them all with orange stakes upon the spot.
The ground is turned upside down, where the anthill once stood, there is now a concrete road.
In between, there are beautiful gardens.
Weeping willows and bare pines. 
Someone built a house, fathered a son, his dreams were too numerous to count.
He planted his shrub in gravel dust.
A heavy fate awaits him. So many sorrowful years.
And it's not as if better times will come after a difficult battle.
Ruin comes and brings along the weight of weariness, the wreck of wounds still healing.
Your groan. Something snapped. It echoed through the years, cut through, struck back, and broke what still remained...
So many winters. A garden of nettles sown. The mole grows frail, the weeds have grown.
A flower to the right, worries to the left. They stand and stare, their sight a blight upon the eyes.
And better days will come, half-shadows fall, heap on manure and spread it over all.
